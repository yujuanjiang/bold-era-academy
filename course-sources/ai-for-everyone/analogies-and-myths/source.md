# Analogies & Myths

Use everyday comparisons to understand AI — and clear up the most common misconceptions.

## Learning Cards

### Card 1: How should I think about AI assistants?

Think of an AI assistant as a helpful first draft partner. It can get you started, but you still review, edit, and decide. It is like a GPS: useful for directions, but you still watch the road.

**Key idea:** AI is a starting point — you still drive.

### Card 2: Why does AI need clear instructions?

AI performs better when you explain the goal, audience, and format. Vague requests often lead to vague results. It is like ordering food: the clearer your order, the closer the result matches what you wanted.

**Key idea:** Clarity in — clarity out.

### Card 3: How can AI be useful but imperfect?

AI can save time and suggest useful ideas, but it can also miss context or make mistakes. It is like a calculator with a sticky key: powerful, but you still check the result.

**Key idea:** Useful does not mean flawless.

### Card 4: Is AI always objective?

No, AI can reflect patterns, gaps, or bias in the data used to train it. A polished answer is not automatically fair or complete.

**Key idea:** Polish is not the same as objectivity.

### Card 5: Does AI know the truth automatically?

No, AI predicts likely answers and may be outdated or mistaken. For important topics, compare with trusted sources.

**Key idea:** Verify before trusting — especially on important topics.
